# Threat Modelling 0.1.0 — release notes

The first release of Threat Modelling, an Obsidian plugin for writing threat models in your notes and seeing them as diagrams, with a report you can print or export to PDF.

## Models

- Write a model in a `threat` code block, or in a `.threat` file of its own: people, systems, containers, components, stores and queues, the trust zones they run in, the flows between them, and the threats to any of them. Mistakes are listed with their line numbers; warnings (such as a threat without an ID) under the view.
- Threats have an ID, STRIDE categories (`#S`, or combined, `#STR`), a status (open, mitigated, accepted, transferred), an owner and a mitigation. One threat can apply to several elements, zones and flows (`on = [...]`).
- Each threat is rated one way: likelihood × impact (1 to 5 each, the main way), a risk tag (`#high`) for a quick rating, or a CVSS 3.x vector for a known vulnerability. Likelihood and impact can be given again for after the threat's controls, for its residual risk. A threat rated more than one way gets a warning.
- Controls record what is done about threats: one control can cover several threats, with a status (planned, implemented, verified), an owner and a due date.
- Hashtags mark sensitive data (`#secret`, `#pii`, `#confidential`). Flows can say whether they are encrypted (or their protocol says so) and authenticated.
- Elements can have icons (`icon = "user"`), from the Lucide set Obsidian already has, with an optional colour and background.
- Model details for the report: version, author, owner, status (draft, in review, approved), review date, scope, what is out of scope, and assumptions.

## Diagrams

- Three views, chosen from a list above each model: a C4-style **Structure** view (at Context, Container or Component level), a **Sequence** view of a chosen scenario, and a **Data flow** view with trust boundaries and numbered flows.
- Boxes and arrows carry their threats' STRIDE letters, each in a box of its own colour, and their threat IDs with each threat's rating, most serious first. Open threats colour the boxes and arrows by risk; closed ones colour nothing and their IDs are struck through. Unencrypted flows are dashed.
- Hover over a threat ID to see the threat; click it to see everywhere that threat applies (the rest of the diagram fades) and find it in the table underneath.
- Under each diagram: a table of the threats it shows (each status in its own colour, an open threat's progress with its controls, and its rating explained when you hover), findings from the model, and a key to the colours. A *Compact* switch shortens the labels.
- Findings from the model: sensitive data on an unencrypted flow, or crossing a zone boundary with nobody saying whether it is encrypted; unauthenticated flows across boundaries or into stores; stores written straight from an untrusted zone.
- Diagrams follow your theme in light and dark mode, and the colours can be changed in a CSS snippet.

## The report

- The **Report** button saves the report as a new note (never replacing one), in a Reports folder beside the model's note or a folder you choose, and opens it in a new tab, ready to print or export to PDF.
- It opens with a summary for readers in a hurry: the open threats by risk, before and after controls, the top three risks, and what needs action. Then the scope and method.
- The threat register lists each threat once, open ones in full and closed ones briefly; everywhere else threats are named by an ID that links to their entry (hover to see it). A CVSS rating is explained in words and links to FIRST's CVSS calculator.
- Then the controls, an action plan grouped by owner (with overdue controls marked), findings from the model, a risk matrix of likelihood against impact before and after controls, the diagrams (their threat IDs linked to the register), the flows that cross a trust boundary and the sensitive data on them, the STRIDE categories not yet considered for each part of the model, and the elements and flows.
- The report note has properties (model, date, version, status, review date, and counts such as open threats by risk, controls by status and overdue controls), so Obsidian's Bases or Dataview can list all your threat models and the state they are in.

## Requirements

Obsidian 1.15 or later, which draws Mermaid diagrams with Pele. Works on desktop and mobile.

## Privacy

Threat Modelling makes no network requests. It writes a file only when you ask: saving a report as a note, or editing a `.threat` file. The links to FIRST's CVSS calculator open your browser only when you click one, and the vector they carry goes after a `#`, which browsers don't send to the website.
