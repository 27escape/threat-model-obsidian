# Threat Modelling 0.2.3 — release notes

The web editor grows into a point-and-click editor, and a fix for diagrams that another plugin could stop from being drawn in Obsidian.

## The web editor

https://27escape.github.io/threat-model-obsidian/

### Point and click

- **Click a box, a zone or an arrow** on a diagram to change it in a panel: a box's name, technology, zone, icon, link and description; a zone's trust; a flow's label, protocol, whether it is encrypted or authenticated, and its data. The panel lists its threats, with a button to add one, and the STRIDE categories usual for what you clicked are marked.
- **Click a threat** (its ID on a diagram, or its row in the threat register) to change it: its title, STRIDE categories, how it is rated (likelihood and impact before and after its controls, a risk tag, or a CVSS vector, checked and explained as you type), status, controls, owner, mitigation and description.
- **Add without typing:** *Add*, at the top, adds an element, a flow, a zone, a threat to the whole model, a control or a scenario. A box's panel has *New flow from it* and, for a system or container, *New element inside*.
- **The *Model* menu** lists the zones, controls and scenarios to change or add. A scenario's steps are chosen from the flows and put in order with up and down.
- **Delete** boxes, zones, flows, threats, controls and scenarios. A box can be deleted only once its flows are gone.
- **Your text is kept as you wrote it.** Every change writes only what changed in the model's text, keeping the rest, comments and layout included. A change that would leave the model with a new error is refused, with the reason.

### Editing

- **Undo and redo** take back any change, typed or made in a form: use the arrows at the top, or Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z (⌘ on a Mac).
- **Line numbers.** Click *Line N* on an error or a warning to go to that line.
- **The problems count**, at the top, opens a list of errors, warnings and findings from the model; each one goes to its line.
- ***Show in text*** selects what you clicked in the model's text.

### Getting it out

- ***Report*** shows the report in the page, to read, print, save as PDF (from the print dialog), or download as Markdown for Obsidian.
- ***Download picture*** saves the diagram shown, as SVG or PNG.
- ***Download threat register*** saves the threats as CSV, for a spreadsheet or a ticket system.
- ***Copy a link to share*** packs the whole model into the link itself. Nothing is uploaded: the model is after the `#`, which browsers never send to a website. Whoever opens the link sees the model and can save a copy of their own.

### Also new

- **Earlier versions:** your own models keep up to 10 earlier versions in the browser (at most one every ten minutes, and one each time you open another model). *Earlier versions…* in the models menu restores one, and that can be undone.
- **Full screen** shows the diagrams alone.
- ***Help*** (or `?`) has the keyboard shortcuts and the whole language reference.
- **Every menu and dialog closes the same way:** Esc, a click outside it, or its ✕.

## Fixed in the plugin

- **Diagrams weren't drawn when another plugin's code-block processing failed.** The plugin used to draw each diagram by having Obsidian render a `mermaid` code block, which runs every plugin's Markdown post-processor on it. One that throws (Embedded Code Title 0.5.5 does) stopped the diagram, the threat register and the key from being drawn. Diagrams are now drawn with Obsidian's own Mermaid directly. If a diagram can't be drawn, its problem is shown in its place, and the rest of the block is still drawn.

## Requirements

Obsidian 1.15 or later. Works on desktop and mobile.

## Privacy

Unchanged: the plugin makes no network requests, and writes a file only when you ask. The web editor makes no connections either: your models stay in your browser, and a share link carries the model in the link itself.
